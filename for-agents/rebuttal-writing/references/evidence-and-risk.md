# Evidence And Risk

## Decision-Risk Rubric

| Risk | Meaning | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Could make the method invalid or the result uninterpretable | Direct proof, correction, decisive control, or explicit withdrawal |
| Major | Threatens novelty, main empirical claim, or generality | Focused argument plus concrete evidence or narrowed scope |
| Moderate | Limits reproducibility, efficiency, or interpretation | Verified detail, comparison, or specific revision |
| Minor | Typo, citation, caption, or local exposition issue | Brief acknowledgement and exact correction |

Treat a low-confidence review as real evidence about clarity, but distinguish
that from a technically established flaw.

## Evidence Strength

Use the strongest applicable level:

1. **Direct decisive evidence:** theorem, controlled experiment, or exact
   correction that resolves the stated concern.
2. **Triangulated evidence:** multiple controls or analyses that jointly rule
   out the reviewer's alternative explanation.
3. **Mechanistic support:** plausible explanation supported by partial
   measurements, clearly labeled as a hypothesis.
4. **Clarification:** resolves a mismatch between the paper and the review but
   does not add empirical support.
5. **Commitment:** improves the manuscript but does not establish a claim.
6. **Future work:** acceptable only after acknowledging the current scope.

Never present levels 3-6 as level 1.

## New-Experiment Checklist

- State the question the experiment answers.
- Identify the changed variable and the controls.
- Report the full comparison, including unfavorable outcomes.
- Give sample size, seeds, or trials.
- Use the same metric and setup unless the change is justified.
- Report baseline tuning effort and inference or training compute when they
  differ materially.
- Avoid cherry-picked tasks or explain task selection.
- For qualitative media, report the sample denominator, selection procedure,
  and failures; preserve an uncurated index when feasible.
- Put decision-critical numbers and settings in the response text. Do not rely
  only on an anonymous site, attachment, or figure that may disappear.
- State whether the result supports, weakens, or leaves the claim unresolved.

## Claim Audit

For each substantive sentence, ask:

- Is it a fact, interpretation, commitment, or hypothesis?
- Can a reviewer locate the supporting evidence?
- Does the evidence establish this scope?
- Is a causal statement being inferred from correlation?
- Is the response moving the goalposts from the submitted claim?
- Would a narrower statement be more defensible?

## Rating Interpretation

Track ratings only to evaluate which tactics correlated with resolution.
Do not treat acceptance or a score increase as proof that every response was
technically correct. Reviewer follow-ups and the final manuscript are stronger
evidence of which concerns were actually resolved.

Do not infer an unrecorded pre-rebuttal score from a displayed final score.
Distinguish a reviewer-authored score change from an area chair's prediction.
