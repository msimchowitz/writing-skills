# Abstract Writing

Write the abstract so that a reader can understand the paper's central
problem, answer, evidence, and demonstrated scope without seeing the rest of
the manuscript. Do not give every section or contribution equal space.

## Read The Evidence First

1. Read the current abstract and the title.
2. Read the introduction, headline results, conclusion, and limitations when
   they are available. Inspect the decisive figure, theorem, or experiment
   behind each abstract claim.
3. Treat the manuscript and any example corpus selected for the task as voice
   evidence. Never infer a result, comparison, number, or limitation from a
   generic abstract pattern.
4. When matching the Simchowitz-group research voice, read
   [example-abstract-patterns.md](references/example-abstract-patterns.md).
5. When no stronger voice sample governs the task, read the sibling
   [academic-voice skill](../academic-voice/SKILL.md).

If only notes are available, distinguish planned work from completed work.
Ask for a missing result when the abstract would otherwise imply evidence that
the notes do not contain.

## Build One Inferential Arc

Before drafting, state these elements in plain language:

1. the concrete problem or successful practice;
2. the limitation, tension, or missing explanation;
3. the paper's central answer;
4. the strongest evidence for that answer;
5. the conditions or limitations that determine its scope; and
6. the change in understanding or design that follows.

Use only the elements needed to understand the argument. Merge adjacent moves
when they express one relation. Reorder them when a result-led opening gives
the reader a clearer path. Do not force a separate limitation sentence when
the comparator, regime, assumptions, or validation setting already makes the
scope clear.

## Draft For Understanding

- Establish the tension before naming a method whose purpose would otherwise
  be unclear.
- Explain the conceptual move before listing algorithmic components.
- Give the headline result enough context to interpret the comparison,
  guarantee, or mechanism.
- Prefer one decisive comparison to a catalog of benchmarks, settings, and
  implementation details.
- Separate direct evidence from a proposed explanation. Use "shows" or
  "demonstrates" for direct evidence, "supports" for partial evidence, and
  "suggests" or "is consistent with" for interpretation.
- End on the supported implication, design consequence, or limitation that
  changes the reader's understanding. Do not end by announcing code, future
  work, or another contribution unless that information matters to the venue.

Avoid wrappers such as "This paper presents," "On the empirical side," "On
the theoretical side," and "Beyond proposing." They describe the document's
inventory instead of explaining the argument. Do not force the draft into a
fixed number of sentences or equal-length contribution blocks.

## Protect The Claim Boundary

Check every sentence against the manuscript:

- Does the abstract name the same comparator, regime, metric, and uncertainty
  as the supporting result?
- Does a mechanism claim remain a hypothesis when the evidence is
  correlational or indirect?
- Does the scope include the conditions needed to interpret the result?
- Do limitations qualify the exact claim they constrain?
- Does the abstract omit interesting details that do not help a reader
  understand the central answer?

Use literal verbs for claims, evidence, and sections. A claim may hold, apply,
depend on an assumption, or have limitations; it does not "stop," "want," or
"do the work."

## Revise The Cadence

Let sentence length follow the argument. A long sentence can hold a claim and
its essential condition when the relation stays clear. A short sentence
should carry real emphasis, not manufacture urgency. Avoid repeated sentence
openings, contribution announcements, and several consecutive sentences with
the same clause structure.

After the claim audit, run the complete sibling
[general-writing workflow](../general-writing/SKILL.md) once. It applies
Humanizer and the shared cadence pass. Recheck every comparison, qualifier,
number, citation, and claim boundary after the prose edit.

## Final Abstract Audit

- Can a reader state the problem and central answer after one reading?
- Does the abstract explain why the intervention or analysis resolves the
  stated tension?
- Is the strongest evidence visible without a benchmark inventory?
- Does each method detail help interpret a result?
- Does the ending state a supported consequence or boundary rather than
  recap the paper?
- Does the paragraph sound continuous when read at speaking pace?
