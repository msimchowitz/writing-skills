# Visual design and review

Use these principles when choosing layouts and when inspecting a rendered deck.
Treat a supplied brand system or approved example deck as the higher-priority
visual authority.

## Visual character

- Keep the composition open. Prefer one strong statement, diagram, equation,
  or piece of evidence over several small panels.
- Use large, readable titles and a clear content hierarchy.
- Use images, screenshots, plots, diagrams, and equations as evidence, not as
  decoration.
- Use color to encode meaning. Once a color represents a concept, keep that
  assignment consistent.
- Maintain stable margins, alignments, line weights, corner treatments, and
  page furniture.
- Correct defects inherited from an example deck. Do not preserve clipping,
  overlap, tiny text, off-canvas objects, or unresolved placeholders.

## Layout families

Build a deck from a small set of recurring slide families:

- title;
- framing question or quotation;
- section divider;
- definition or technical setup;
- conceptual diagram;
- derivation or progressive build;
- experiment, result, or evidence;
- comparison;
- synthesis or conclusion.

Choose the family that serves the slide's job. Repetition should create rhythm,
not force unlike content into the same shape.

## Writing and pacing

- Give each slide one main job.
- Keep visible text short and concrete.
- Use bullets only for a true list. Prefer spatial structure, a diagram, or a
  short statement for relationships and arguments.
- Introduce one meaningful change per progressive build.
- Pair an equation with a plain account of what changes, what stays fixed, and
  why the step matters.
- Reserve labels such as "claim" for genuinely disputable statements. Use
  "definition," "observation," "result," or the relevant theorem label when
  accurate.
- Make section dividers announce topics. Reveal conclusions after the material
  that supports them unless the narrative requires front-loading.
- Remove AI-sounding filler, importance puffery, generic transitions, and
  summary-recap endings from titles, body text, labels, captions, and notes.

## Rendered-deck checklist

Inspect every slide and every overlay at full-slide size. Confirm that:

1. No text, equation, image, or shape is clipped or outside the canvas.
2. No objects overlap unless the overlap is intentional and legible.
3. Body text and citations are readable at presentation distance.
4. Titles fit their containers and state the slide's point or role.
5. Repeated builds preserve prior steps and add one useful visible step.
6. Figures, equations, facts, and citations match the source.
7. Mathematical notation uses the intended superscripts, subscripts, primes,
   limits, and conditioning bars.
8. Images retain their aspect ratio and useful content is not cropped away.
9. Color assignments, margins, typography, and page furniture stay consistent.
10. No placeholder text, template residue, duplicate slides, or hidden source
    content remains.
11. The final deck remains editable or retains compilable source.
12. The rendered artifact opens and contains the expected number of slides.

Do not approve a deck solely because automated checks pass. Visual inspection
of the rendered output is required.
