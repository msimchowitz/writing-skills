Agent prose can be grammatically clean and still feel wrong. Often, the missing
element is a particular reader. The answer works in general, but no one seems
to need this exact sentence. Ordinary facts get inflated. Uncertainty
disappears, while minor and major points receive the same weight. This guide
explains those habits and shows revisions that preserve the writer's facts and
voice. The appendix keeps the complete pattern catalog for reference.

A later chapter asks why these patterns recur. It starts with next-token
prediction and generic audience models, then considers mixed training data.
Code and documentation receive separate attention because they may shape prose
differently. The chapter closes with post-training incentives. These are
hypotheses, not findings. The patterns are useful for revision; they do not
establish authorship.
