Fluent prose can feel generic even when every sentence is grammatical. When no
reader is specified, agent-generated text can give routine background and
decisive evidence equal weight, then recast unresolved uncertainty in familiar
formulas. Human writers also use these formulas, and language agents can
produce precise, situated prose, so surface patterns cannot identify
authorship. These patterns are better understood as properties of passages and
as clues that motivate hypotheses about the pressures shaping model output.
Revision therefore begins by identifying the intended reader and recovering
the relations among a claim, its evidence, and the conditions under which it
holds.
Explanations based on audience specification, training, or local prediction
remain hypotheses. A successful revision makes those relations easier to
understand without erasing the writer's judgment or changing the strength of
the claim.
