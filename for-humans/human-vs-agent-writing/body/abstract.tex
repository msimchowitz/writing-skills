Agent prose can be difficult to follow even when every sentence is grammatical
and the document looks finished. Strong human writing reflects judgments made
for a particular reader: it gives ideas unequal weight and states the
relations among claims, evidence, and scope. When some of those decisions
remain unresolved, agent prose often relies on reusable forms instead. The
result may feel choppy when rhythm gives unlike ideas similar emphasis, or
sloppy when fluent sentences blur agency and evidential force. Voice also
weakens when the prose settles into a portable middle register. Possible
explanations include local prediction, missing reader context, and training
that rewards visible completeness, but these remain hypotheses. The surface
patterns do not establish authorship: people can rely on the same defaults, and
agents can produce precise, situated prose. The writing skills developed to
correct these problems converge on a common standard: choices of detail,
syntax, and emphasis should follow from the argument and the reader's needs.
