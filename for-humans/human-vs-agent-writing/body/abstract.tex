This guide is for writers and editors reviewing prose that feels polished but
generic or detached from a particular reader. It treats recurring agent-like
habits as editing problems, not evidence about authorship. Use the patterns to
locate places where a draft inflates a claim or organizes material
mechanically. The same review can expose vague assumptions about the audience.
Revisions should preserve supported facts and uncertainty while leaving the
writer's deliberate voice intact. The examples and checklist work as a
line-editing aid or a final review before publication.
