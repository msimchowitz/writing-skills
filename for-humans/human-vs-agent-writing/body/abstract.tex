Fluent prose can feel generic even when every sentence is grammatical. When no
reader is specified, agent-generated text can give routine background the same
weight as decisive evidence and recast unresolved uncertainty in familiar
formulas. Because human writers also use these formulas and language agents can
produce precise, situated prose, surface patterns cannot identify authorship.
They are more useful as properties of passages and as evidence about the
pressures shaping model output. Revision begins with the intended reader and
the semantic relations made explicit in the passage; causal explanations based
on audience specification, training, or local prediction remain hypotheses.
The central task is to recover the relation among a claim, its evidence, and
the conditions under which it holds. Once that structure is visible, cadence
can follow the argument.
