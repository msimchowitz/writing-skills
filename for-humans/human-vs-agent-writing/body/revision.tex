\section{A five-pass revision}
\label{sec:revision}

Edit the document in passes so that a style change does not quietly become a
factual change.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Read the whole draft. Note its central point, intended reader,
    factual boundary, and a few signs of the writer's voice.

    \item Protect the source material. Mark names, numbers, quotations,
    citations, technical distinctions, and uncertainty before rewriting.

    \item Work at paragraph scale. Find clusters of formulaic language, put
    the decisive claim at the point needed by the reader, and group details by
    their relation to that claim.

    \item Work at sentence scale. Locate the subject and verb, state direct
    relations, keep the head noun in focus, and name an actor when agency
    matters.

    \item Compare the result with the source, restore any detail or cadence
    lost in cleanup, and read the document aloud. Remove chat residue and
    decorative endings only after the meaning is secure.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{A short worked example}

\agentdraft

Effective writing is not just about clarity; it is about fostering connection,
enhancing engagement, and ensuring that ideas resonate. By leveraging a
reader-centered approach, writers can create robust communication that truly
makes an impact.

\humanrevision

Effective writing gives a particular reader the information needed to
understand or use an idea.

The first draft piles a canned contrast, a forced group of three, promotional
language, and a corporate verb onto a simple idea. It invokes the reader
without naming a need. The revision names that need and stops.

This example does not imply that every paragraph should collapse into one
sentence. Detail should remain when it supplies evidence, scope, mechanism, or
voice. The point is to remove language that performs significance without
carrying information.

An editor can remove so much irregularity that the writer disappears. Stop
before the document becomes yours. The finished version should still contain
the writer's facts, judgment, and cadence. Leave strong sentences alone, even
when you would have written them differently.
