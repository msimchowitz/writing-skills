\section{Revision as constrained inference}
\label{sec:revision}

Style revision is safest when it proceeds from the document's evidence and
reader model toward its sentences. The order matters because a local
improvement can quietly change a factual claim, while a broad cleanup can
erase the cadence that made the passage recognizably the writer's.

\subsection{The source sets the factual boundary}

A conservative revision starts from the draft's central point and intended
reader. Its factual boundary and recurring voice constrain the possible
rewrites; calibrated uncertainty does as well. Changing ``suggests'' to
``shows'' changes the claim even when the sentence becomes shorter.

\subsection{Paragraphs expose the hierarchy of ideas}

Clusters of formulaic language often reveal a paragraph whose governing
relation is missing. At paragraph scale, the editor can identify the decisive
claim and group evidence according to the role it plays. Evidence for a result,
qualifications that specify its extent, and mechanistic details that explain
why it may hold play different roles. Ordering them accordingly lets readers
recover those dependencies without assigning every sentence the same visual
or rhythmic weight.

\subsection{Sentences expose agency and relation}

Sentence-level analysis identifies the grammatical subject and verb, then
tests the logical role of each modifier. At this scale, unnamed actors,
mechanical relative clauses, and mismatched verbs become visible. Cadence also
requires attention here, although the relevant pattern usually spans several
sentences rather than one.

\subsection{Comparison with the source reveals losses}

Comparing the revised passage with the source may reveal a specific detail,
qualification, or abrupt transition lost during cleanup. It also helps
distinguish chat residue and decorative endings from genuine voice. Reading at
speaking pace makes repeated clause shapes audible, but the source remains the
best evidence for the writer's deliberate irregularities.

\subsection{A compact example}

\agentdraft

Effective writing is not just about clarity; it is about fostering connection,
enhancing engagement, and ensuring that ideas resonate. By leveraging a
reader-centered approach, writers can create robust communication that truly
makes an impact.

\humanrevision

Effective writing gives a particular reader the information needed to
understand or use an idea.

The first draft combines a canned contrast with a forced group of three.
Promotional language and a corporate verb add ceremony to a simple claim,
while the reference to readers never identifies their need. The revision
states that need directly.

This degree of compression would be wrong if the paragraph contained evidence,
scope, or a mechanism. It works here because the discarded phrases repeat one
unsupported abstraction. Revision can remove that repetition without
establishing a general preference for one-sentence paragraphs.

The limiting case is an edit so regular that the writer disappears. Success is
better measured by whether the writer's facts, judgment, and recognizable
cadence remain, even where another editor would have made a different choice.
