\section{Visible organization}
\label{sec:structure}

Headings, lists, summaries, and balanced sections make a document look
finished. They are also easy for an evaluator to recognize. Agent prose often
uses these signals before the relations among its claims are clear. The layout
can then give equal weight to ideas that play very different roles.

\subsection{Headings can expose or conceal the argument}

A useful heading marks a real change in the reader's task. Tiny sections and
headings that merely restate their first sentence create navigation without
reducing uncertainty. Bold label-and-colon bullets have a similar effect:
connected claims begin to resemble independent interface fields.

Human writers also use headings and bullets, but the form follows the reading
task. A fixed procedure benefits from visible steps; a reference taxonomy
benefits from parallel entries. An explanation usually needs connected prose
because the relation among its claims matters more than their count.

\subsection{Abstracts show the difference sharply}

\agentdraft

This article presents a comprehensive framework for human and agent writing.
It discusses reader models, examines sentence structure, and explores cadence.
It also provides a taxonomy and directions for future work.

\humanrevision

Agent prose can remain fluent while flattening emphasis and loosening semantic
relations. Comparing passages shows that its choppiness and sloppiness often
arise when reusable patterns replace judgment about a particular reader.

The first abstract tours the document. Its sentences are accurate as an
inventory, but a reader finishes without knowing the article's answer. The
human revision uses the same space to establish the phenomenon and explain it
through a direct comparison.

This difference matters because an abstract is a person's first encounter with
the argument. A strong abstract does not give every section equal space. It
selects the problem, answer, decisive evidence, and claim boundary needed to
understand the work without reading the paper.

\subsection{Paragraphs often end by convention}

An agent-generated paragraph may append a slogan, broad implication, or
summary because the local form suggests that a conclusion is due. The sentence
creates tonal completion even when the reasoning ended one line earlier.

\agentdraft

The method improves accuracy only when labeled examples are available.
Together, these findings provide valuable insight and open exciting directions
for future research.

\humanrevision

The method improves accuracy only when labeled examples are available.

The limitation is the natural ending because it determines the extent of the
result. The generic final sentence shifts attention from that boundary to
unsupported uplift. A human paragraph may also end on an unresolved tension, a
concrete fact, or a transition that the next paragraph genuinely needs. It
need not sound conclusive merely because it ends.

\subsection{Formatting cannot supply hierarchy}

Mechanical boldface, title case everywhere, decorative icons, and dense bullet
lists can accompany agent writing, but none identifies its author. They become
problems when the visual hierarchy disagrees with the argument. Typography can
help a reader locate structure; it cannot create the structure that the prose
has not established.
