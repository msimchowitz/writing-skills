\section{Fidelity sets the limits of revision}
\label{sec:principles}

A successful revision changes the reading experience without changing the
writer's claim. Style remains subordinate to factual fidelity: names, numbers,
dates, and quotations remain grounded in the source or the writer. The same
constraint protects citations and technical distinctions. When the draft
lacks a needed fact, vivid detail cannot fill the gap.

Fiction differs because invention belongs to the form. In factual prose, an
unsupported sensory detail remains a fabrication no matter how natural it
sounds. Technical, legal, and reference writing impose the same boundary.

\subsection{Uncertainty is part of the claim}

The verbs ``demonstrates,'' ``suggests,'' ``is consistent with,'' and ``does
not rule out'' encode different relations between evidence and conclusion.
Compression becomes distortion when it turns a conditional result into a
universal one. Confidence and ambivalence require similar protection: a
writer's hesitation may be the most accurate account of the evidence.

Missing evidence also constrains what the passage can say. If the available
sources do not establish a founding date, the passage can acknowledge that
absence when it matters or leave the date unmentioned. A plausible biography,
motivation, or causal history would fill the gap with material that the sources
do not support.

\subsection{The choice of detail carries voice}

Voice appears in the choice of detail as much as in punctuation. One writer
may retain a technical qualification that interrupts the rhythm because the
qualification prevents an overclaim. Another may pause for a dated joke or
correct an earlier thought in a parenthesis. Regularizing either choice can
make the prose smoother and less faithful at the same time.

An earlier sample from the same writer provides stronger evidence about
diction and cadence than a generic style rule. Such samples also reveal that
voice is often uneven: a long explanation may give way to a short conclusion,
while an aside interrupts an otherwise formal register. That unevenness can be
part of the voice, especially when the writer leaves a genuine tension
unresolved.

\subsection{The smallest sufficient intervention}

The scale of revision follows the scale of the defect. A confused modifier may
need a local repair, whereas a paragraph that hides its central comparison may
need a new order. Anecdotes and detours survive when they create stakes or
reveal judgment. Reorganization becomes warranted when the existing sequence
obscures priority, repeats the same point, or forces the reader to reconstruct
a dependency.

Limiting the edit in this way leaves strong sentences intact and prevents the
editor's preferred cadence from replacing the writer's merely because both
are defensible.

\subsection{Sentence function depends on the reader}

A sentence can contribute a claim, condition, example, transition, or moment
of voice. Its value lies in the understanding or inference it makes possible,
not in an abstract requirement to ``earn its place.'' Throat-clearing and
empty qualifiers fail because they postpone that contribution. Generic
encouragement has the same effect when it replaces information the reader
needs.
