\section{Unequal weight}
\label{sec:principles}

A human writer assigns available facts unequal weight. Detail is selected
according to the reader's problem, then arranged so that evidence,
qualification, and explanation remain subordinate to the claim they serve.
Common agent prose often begins with coverage and visible completeness,
leaving the reader to infer the hierarchy of ideas.

\subsection{The reader determines which details matter}

The same subject produces different passages for different readers. A
collaborator choosing between methods needs the governing tradeoff early. A
reviewer may care first about the control that makes the comparison credible.
A broad audience may need neither detail until the practical question is
clear. Good human prose reveals these assumptions through what it explains and
what it leaves compressed.

Agent prose often defaults to details that travel well across contexts:
background, definitions, broad benefits, balanced caveats, and a concluding
summary. None is inherently wrong. The problem is that their inclusion may
follow a familiar document shape rather than a judgment about this reader.

\subsection{Catalogs flatten the argument}

\agentdraft

The report covers architecture, data collection, evaluation metrics,
deployment constraints, and future work. Each section offers key insights and
important considerations.

\humanrevision

The report asks whether the system is ready for deployment. Its account of the
architecture and data supports that judgment; the evaluation supplies the
deciding evidence. Future work concerns the questions that the evidence leaves
open.

The agent draft inventories the document and assigns every topic the same
status. The human revision supplies a governing question, then gives the
remaining material distinct roles. It is more informative even though it names
fewer categories, because the reader can see why each one appears.

Lists remain useful when readers need complete membership. Procedures and fixed
taxonomies are meant to be scanned, as are option sets. Connected claims depend
instead on cause, condition, contrast, or consequence, and prose must make that
relation legible.

\subsection{Paragraphs reveal a hierarchy of claims}

A strong paragraph develops toward a particular inference. Familiar context
may lead to a claim and its evidence before the paragraph ends on the
consequence or boundary that the evidence supports. Not every paragraph needs
all of these moves, and their order changes with the argument. What remains
stable is the distinction among their functions.

Agent paragraphs often remain grammatical while losing this hierarchy. A
claim, an example, a caveat, and a broad implication arrive in consecutive
sentences of similar size. The reader sees four grammatical units but has to
reconstruct the dependency among them. Adding bullets or bold labels makes the
units easier to scan without resolving that problem.

\subsection{Emphasis follows evidence}

Good human writing usually earns emphasis through evidence. Agent prose more
readily declares importance in advance: events become ``pivotal,'' designs
``underscore'' commitments, and methods ``unlock'' possibilities. These words
create a mood of significance while leaving the basis of the evaluation
unclear.

Observable facts give the reader a basis for evaluation that unsupported
praise cannot. ``The institution opened the archive to the public and placed
the catalog online'' names two such changes. Calling the archive a
transformative milestone adds ceremony but no evidence. If a date, access
policy, or catalog size matters, the source must supply it; praise cannot stand
in for the missing fact.
