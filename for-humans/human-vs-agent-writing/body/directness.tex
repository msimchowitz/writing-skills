\section{Generic emphasis competes with evidence}
\label{sec:directness}

Agent-like prose can declare a subject important before giving the reader
evidence for that judgment. Ordinary events become ``pivotal moments,'' a town
``boasts'' features, and a design ``underscores'' a commitment. Such phrases
ask the reader to accept an evaluation that the sentence has not yet
supported.

\agentdraft

The new archive marks a pivotal moment in the institution's evolving
landscape, underscoring its enduring commitment to accessible scholarship.

\humanrevision

The institution opened the archive to the public and placed the catalog
online.

The revision replaces an evaluation with two observable changes. A date,
catalog size, or access policy could make the account more useful if the
source supplied it; absent that evidence, another compliment would only
restate the original problem.

\subsection{Direct verbs expose the relation}

Abstract wrappers postpone the action. Phrases such as ``the mechanism behind
X'' or ``the role of Y in Z'' identify a relationship without specifying it.
A direct verb reveals whether X causes, constrains, predicts, or merely
correlates with Y, provided the evidence supports that relation.

Verb specificity also depends on the subject. A claim can hold under stated
conditions and imply consequences; motion toward a stopping point describes
neither relation. Conditions do not simply ``limit the claim,'' either,
because that wording leaves the affected dimension unnamed. A validity
statement names the conditions under which a claim holds, whereas an
implication statement specifies how far its consequences extend. Sometimes the
sentence needs an intermediate noun such as \emph{extent},
\emph{applicability}, or \emph{consequence} before the verb can express the
relation accurately.

Simple copulas are often sufficient. ``Is'' and ``has'' can be clearer than
``serves as,'' ``stands as,'' or ``boasts,'' while ``can process'' states the
same relation more directly than ``has the ability to process.'' A more
forceful verb helps only when it describes a more specific action.

\subsection{Attribution determines checkability}

Phrases such as ``experts argue'' and ``studies show'' borrow authority from
unnamed sources. Once an expert, study, dataset, or observation is identified,
the reader can inspect the basis of the claim. Without such support, the
passage can state only the narrower fact established by the evidence.

Lists of media outlets create a related effect when they substitute notability
for substance. A citation matters because of the claim it supports. One source
tied to a specific claim is therefore more informative than a catalog of
publication names.

\subsection{Mechanisms replace ceremonial interpretation}

Trailing participles such as ``highlighting,'' ``showcasing,'' and
``underscoring'' can append an interpretation without explaining why it
follows. A mechanism, comparison, or consequence supplies the missing
relation.

\agentdraft

The interface uses blue and green, reflecting the team's commitment to trust
and growth while fostering a more engaging experience.

\humanrevision

The interface uses blue for selected controls and green for successful
actions.

The revision assigns each color a function. If the source establishes only the
colors, even that explanation would be too strong; the supported description
would then end after ``blue and green.''

\subsection{Concrete endings preserve scope}

Generic conclusions promise a bright future, celebrate a journey, or declare
a step in the right direction. They change the mood without changing the
reader's understanding. A supported result often provides the natural ending,
especially when its final clause identifies a limitation. In a document
organized around a decision, closure may instead come from stating the
decision that remains.
