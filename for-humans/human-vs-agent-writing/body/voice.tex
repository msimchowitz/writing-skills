\section{Keep the writer in the prose}
\label{sec:voice}

Situated attention is a stronger human signal than a typo. The writer notices
the detail, tension, or exception that matters to this subject and reader.

\subsection{Specific details resist substitution}

Names, measurements, dates, odd quotations, and concrete observations resist
generic paraphrase. Protect them when the source supports them. Agents often
round a specific fact into broad significance; an overzealous editor can make
the same mistake while trying to improve flow.

Specificity does not license invention. Do not add a vivid anecdote, opinion,
or sensory detail merely to make a passage sound human. The useful detail must
belong to the writer or the evidence.

\subsection{Tension can remain unresolved}

People often hold mixed views. A writer may think a policy is mostly useful
and still distrust one consequence. A study may produce a strong result under
one condition and an unexplained failure under another. Preserve that tension
when the evidence does not settle it.

Agent-like prose tends to close the loop. It converts uncertainty into a neat
lesson, adds a positive final paragraph, or balances every criticism with an
optimistic future. A human revision can stop at the unresolved fact.

\subsection{Defensible choices carry voice}

A distinctive word, abrupt transition, or parenthetical aside may look
inefficient in isolation. Keep it when the writer can explain its effect on
the reader. Deliberate choices create voice. Random irregularity does not.

Era-bound references, local language, and field-specific phrasing may also be
part of the voice. Preserve them when the intended audience understands them.
Explain them when the audience does not. Do not replace them with generic
internet language.

\subsection{Polish does not imply automation}

Perfect grammar, formal vocabulary, a dry register, or a consistent style can
all come from a human author or editor. So can a letter-style greeting, a
common transition, a dash, and an isolated short sentence. Unsourced prose is
a research problem, not an authorship test. Correct formatting may come from a
template.

Secondhand language needs special care. A suspicious phrase inside a
quotation, title, proper name, or example belongs to its source. Do not
``humanize'' quoted material. Explain or cite it as needed while preserving
the words.

Version history is stronger evidence than stylistic guesswork. Text committed
before the public release of ChatGPT on November 30, 2022, cannot have been
written with ChatGPT at that time. Provenance, drafts, and revision records
should outweigh a detector's impression of the final prose.

\subsection{Personality depends on the genre}

Essays and personal writing can support humor, first person, and open
uncertainty. Technical or legal prose may need a neutral voice. A humanizing
pass should match the genre instead of adding personality everywhere.
