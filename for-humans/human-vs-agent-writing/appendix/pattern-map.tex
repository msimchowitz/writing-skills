\section{Pattern taxonomy}
\label{app:patterns}

This appendix preserves the complete Humanizer catalog in compact form. The
numbers match version 2.15.0 of the skill. Each entry describes a recurrent
surface pattern and the relation that a grounded revision recovers; the main
text develops the principles shared across entries.

\subsection{Inflated or unsupported content}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Undue emphasis on significance.} Claims about pivotal
    moments, legacies, and broader trends displace the event and its supported
    consequence.

    \item \textbf{Notability and media lists.} Publication names function as
    borrowed importance when the passage never explains which cited claim is
    relevant.

    \item \textbf{Superficial \texttt{-ing} analysis.} Trailing participles
    such as ``highlighting'' and ``showcasing'' append interpretation without
    a mechanism, comparison, or result.

    \item \textbf{Promotional language.} Travel-copy adjectives,
    superlatives, and verbs such as ``boasts'' substitute evaluation for an
    observable property.

    \item \textbf{Vague attribution.} ``Experts argue'' and ``studies show''
    invoke an apparent source while leaving the claim impossible to check.

    \item \textbf{Formulaic challenge and outlook sections.} A generic
    obstacle followed by optimism creates closure without identifying the
    specific constraint, response, or unresolved problem.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Lexical and grammatical defaults}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,start=7]
    \item \textbf{Overused agent vocabulary.} Recurrent words such as
    ``pivotal,'' ``intricate,'' ``landscape,'' ``tapestry,'' and
    ``underscore'' often carry tone after their descriptive content has
    disappeared.

    \item \textbf{Copula avoidance.} ``Serves as,'' ``stands as,''
    ``features,'' and ``boasts'' can make a simple relation less direct than
    ``is'' or ``has.''

    \item \textbf{Negative parallelism and tailing negation.} ``Not only,''
    ``not just,'' and clipped endings such as ``no guessing'' turn a positive
    claim into a reusable contrast frame.

    \item \textbf{Rule-of-three overuse.} A triad sounds complete even when
    the subject does not have three relevant parts.

    \item \textbf{Synonym cycling.} Rotating near-synonyms for one stable
    object creates the appearance of distinctions that the argument does not
    make.

    \item \textbf{False ranges.} ``From X to Y'' implies a scale or
    progression when X and Y are merely separate topics.

    \item \textbf{Passive voice and subjectless fragments.} Agency disappears
    when a responsible actor matters, while fragments such as ``No
    configuration needed'' can leave the grammatical subject implicit.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Punctuation and visible formatting}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,start=14]
    \item \textbf{Dash-heavy rhythm.} Habitual dashes assign the same
    parenthetical stress across unrelated sentences. A writer's established
    use of dashes remains relevant counterevidence.

    \item \textbf{Mechanical boldface.} Repeated emphasis creates a visual
    hierarchy that may have no counterpart in the argument.

    \item \textbf{Inline-header lists.} Bold label-and-colon bullets make
    connected prose resemble a set of independent interface fields.

    \item \textbf{Title case everywhere.} Uniform title case makes every
    heading equally prominent even when the venue or hierarchy calls for a
    quieter convention.

    \item \textbf{Decorative emoji.} Icons attached to headings or bullets add
    salience without carrying information.

    \item \textbf{Curly quotation marks as a default tell.} Curly marks arise
    from typographic systems as readily as from language models and therefore
    carry almost no provenance information on their own.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Chat residue and missing evidence}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,start=20]
    \item \textbf{Collaborative chat artifacts.} ``Of course,'' ``I hope this
    helps,'' and offers to continue preserve dialogue inside a document that
    no longer has a conversational turn structure.

    \item \textbf{Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers and gap-filling.} A missing
    fact becomes a paragraph about unavailable information, sometimes
    followed by a plausible story unsupported by the sources.

    \item \textbf{Sycophantic tone.} Praise for the question or automatic
    agreement establishes rapport without evaluating the substance.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Filler, hedging, and endings}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,start=23]
    \item \textbf{Filler phrases.} ``In order to,'' ``due to the fact that,''
    and ``has the ability to'' delay relations that ``to,'' ``because,'' and
    ``can'' express directly.

    \item \textbf{Excessive hedging.} Several weak qualifiers obscure which
    single modal or condition matches the evidence.

    \item \textbf{Generic positive conclusions.} Bright futures, journeys,
    and steps in the right direction add mood after the final supported
    result, limitation, or decision.

    \item \textbf{Uniform hyphenation.} A compound remains hyphenated in
    predicate position even when ordinary grammar or the publication's
    convention would drop the hyphen.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Rhetorical and structural formulas}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,start=27]
    \item \textbf{Persuasive authority tropes.} ``The real question'' and
    ``at its core'' claim interpretive authority before stating the question
    or relation.

    \item \textbf{Signposting announcements.} ``Let's dive in'' and ``Now we
    will explore'' announce content that could already occupy the sentence.

    \item \textbf{Generic, fragmented, or overbuilt headings.} Vague display
    text, heading restatements, and tiny sections create visible structure
    without clarifying the argument.

    \item \textbf{Diff-anchored writing.} Descriptions of the latest edit make
    a stable document depend on a version transition that matters only in
    changelogs, release notes, and migration guides.

    \item \textbf{Manufactured punchlines and staccato drama.} Stacked
    fragments assign dramatic closure to each line rather than explaining the
    relation that makes a change important.

    \item \textbf{Aphorism formulas.} Constructions such as ``X is the
    language of Y'' offer memorability while leaving the underlying effect
    unspecified.

    \item \textbf{Conversational rhetorical openers.} ``Honestly?'' and
    similar fake-candid pauses delay an ordinary answer in order to stage a
    reveal.

    \item \textbf{Question-word clause wrappers.} Non-question ``what,''
    ``how,'' or ``why'' clauses postpone a known object or relation that could
    appear directly.

    \item \textbf{Loose catalogs and list sprawl.} Series without a governing
    relation distribute equal weight across items whose roles differ.
    Adjacent inventory sentences preserve the same rhythm even across a
    paragraph break.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Sentence focus, reader purpose, and verb semantics}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,start=36]
    \item \textbf{Mechanical relative clauses.} A locally grammatical
    relative clause can force an unnatural passive or obscure the purpose of
    the head noun.

    \item \textbf{Readerless corporate shorthand.} Metaphors of jobs,
    ownership, and deliverables, including the demand that a sentence ``earn
    its place,'' sound decisive while obscuring the passage's actual relation
    to its reader. In practice, the passage may be supplying evidence for an
    inference or information needed for a decision.

    \item \textbf{Semantically mismatched relations.} Claims that ``stop'' and
    sections that ``know'' receive actions their subjects cannot perform.
    Replacing the verb may still leave its object underspecified: conditions
    limit a claim's extent, while a theorem's consequences need not extend
    beyond a stated regime.

    \item \textbf{Mechanical paragraph endings.} A paragraph fades out on
    setup or housekeeping, or appends a vague sentence merely to sound
    complete. Natural closure comes from the supported consequence, boundary,
    unresolved tension, or concrete fact at which the reasoning arrives.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Additional general-writing checks}

General writing compares the broad cleanup with the source. This lets the
editor restore a technical distinction, calibrated uncertainty, or
recognizable cadence removed by an otherwise fluent revision. Specific facts
and deliberate irregularities receive the same protection.

Binary contrasts, negative lists, throat-clearing, and faux-insight claims all
delay a direct point, although a scientific contrast may remain essential to
the claim. Colon reveals and rhetorical questions can manufacture drama, while
repeated sentence shapes make unrelated ideas appear equivalent.

Fake-profound kickers and summary paragraphs create closure without advancing
the argument. A quieter failure occurs when a paragraph ends on setup after
its main point has already appeared. Anecdotes and detours behave differently
when they establish context or tension. The distinction depends on the reading
effect of the passage, not on a blanket preference for compression.
