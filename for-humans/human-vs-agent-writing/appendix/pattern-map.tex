\section{Pattern map}
\label{app:patterns}

This appendix preserves the complete Humanizer catalog in compact form. The
numbers match version 2.12.0 of the skill. Each item names a recurring pattern
and the corresponding revision. The main chapters explain the shared
principles behind the catalog.

\subsection{Inflated or unsupported content}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Undue emphasis on significance.} Replace claims about
    pivotal moments, legacies, and broader trends with the event and its
    supported consequence.

    \item \textbf{Notability and media lists.} Give the context that makes one
    citation relevant instead of using publication names as proof of
    importance.

    \item \textbf{Superficial \texttt{-ing} analysis.} Replace trailing
    participles such as ``highlighting'' and ``showcasing'' with a mechanism,
    comparison, or result.

    \item \textbf{Promotional language.} Remove travel-copy adjectives,
    superlatives, and claims that an object ``boasts'' a feature. State the
    observable fact.

    \item \textbf{Vague attribution.} Name the source behind ``experts argue''
    or ``studies show.'' Narrow or remove an unsupported claim.

    \item \textbf{Formulaic challenge and outlook sections.} Replace a generic
    obstacle-plus-optimism ending with the specific constraint, response, or
    unresolved problem.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Lexical and grammatical defaults}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,start=7]
    \item \textbf{Overused agent vocabulary.} Prefer ordinary, precise words
    to recurrent terms such as ``pivotal,'' ``intricate,'' ``landscape,''
    ``tapestry,'' and ``underscore'' when those terms add no meaning.

    \item \textbf{Copula avoidance.} Use ``is'' and ``has'' when ``serves as,''
    ``stands as,'' ``features,'' or ``boasts'' only makes the sentence longer.

    \item \textbf{Negative parallelism and tailing negation.} State the point
    directly instead of using ``not only,'' ``not just,'' or a clipped ending
    such as ``no guessing.''

    \item \textbf{Rule-of-three overuse.} Keep three-part groups only when the
    subject has three relevant parts.

    \item \textbf{Synonym cycling.} Repeat the stable term instead of rotating
    through near-synonyms for variety.

    \item \textbf{False ranges.} Use ``from X to Y'' only when X and Y define
    a meaningful scale or progression.

    \item \textbf{Passive voice and subjectless fragments.} Name the actor
    when agency matters, and turn fragments into sentences when a reader needs
    the subject.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Punctuation and visible formatting}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,start=14]
    \item \textbf{Dash-heavy rhythm.} Replace habitual dashes with periods,
    commas, colons, parentheses, or a new sentence. Match a supplied author
    sample when dashes are part of that voice.

    \item \textbf{Mechanical boldface.} Reserve emphasis for a genuine
    hierarchy or a term that the publication requires.

    \item \textbf{Inline-header lists.} Turn bold label-and-colon bullets into
    prose unless readers need to scan independent fields.

    \item \textbf{Title case everywhere.} Use the heading capitalization
    required by the venue; sentence case is usually quieter.

    \item \textbf{Decorative emoji.} Remove icons that decorate headings or
    bullets without carrying information.

    \item \textbf{Curly quotation marks as a default tell.} Match the
    document's typographic convention. Curly marks alone say nothing about
    authorship.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Chat residue and missing evidence}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,start=20]
    \item \textbf{Collaborative chat artifacts.} Remove ``Of course,'' ``I
    hope this helps,'' offers to continue, and other dialogue left inside a
    finished document.

    \item \textbf{Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers and gap-filling.} State that a
    source does not establish a fact, or omit the passage. Do not convert
    missing evidence into a plausible story.

    \item \textbf{Sycophantic tone.} Respond to the substance rather than
    praising the question or agreeing automatically.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Filler, hedging, and endings}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,start=23]
    \item \textbf{Filler phrases.} Replace ``in order to,'' ``due to the fact
    that,'' and ``has the ability to'' with ``to,'' ``because,'' and ``can.''

    \item \textbf{Excessive hedging.} Use the one qualification that matches
    the evidence.

    \item \textbf{Generic positive conclusions.} End on the last concrete
    result, limitation, decision, or next action.

    \item \textbf{Uniform hyphenation.} Hyphenate a compound when the
    publication and grammatical position require it. Do not copy an
    attributive hyphen mechanically into predicate position.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Rhetorical and structural formulas}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,start=27]
    \item \textbf{Persuasive authority tropes.} Remove ``the real question,''
    ``at its core,'' and similar ceremony. State the question or claim.

    \item \textbf{Signposting announcements.} Replace ``Let's dive in'' and
    ``Now we will explore'' with the content.

    \item \textbf{Fragmented headers.} Remove the one-line warm-up that merely
    repeats a heading, and merge sections that do not support navigation.

    \item \textbf{Diff-anchored writing.} Describe the current system unless
    the document is a changelog, release note, or migration guide.

    \item \textbf{Manufactured punchlines and staccato drama.} Combine stacked
    fragments and explain the reason a change matters.

    \item \textbf{Aphorism formulas.} Replace ``X is the language of Y'' and
    similar slogans with the concrete relation.

    \item \textbf{Conversational rhetorical openers.} Remove fake-candid
    hooks such as ``Honestly?'' and give the answer directly.

    \item \textbf{Question-word clause wrappers.} Replace non-question
    ``what,'' ``how,'' or ``why'' clauses with a direct noun or statement when
    the relation is known.

    \item \textbf{List sprawl.} Group connected ideas in prose. Keep a long
    list only when it represents a complete set that readers need to scan or
    count.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Global focus and reader purpose}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,start=36]
    \item \textbf{Relative-clause subject drift.} Follow the head noun across
    the full clause. Keep it in focus unless a new subject identifies an
    important actor or bears responsibility.

    \item \textbf{Readerless corporate shorthand.} Replace instructions about
    jobs, ownership, deliverables, or earning a place with the understanding,
    evidence, decision, or action that the prose supports.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Additional general-writing checks}

The general-writing skill adds several broader editing checks around the
Humanizer catalog:

Compare a broad cleanup with the source before accepting it. Restore any lost
technical distinction, calibrated uncertainty, specific fact, or recognizable
cadence.

Cut canned setups such as binary contrasts, negative lists, throat-clearing,
faux-insight claims, colon reveals, and rhetorical questions. Repeated
sentence shapes also deserve attention when they make unrelated ideas appear
equivalent.

Delete fake-profound metaphors, aphoristic kickers, and summary paragraphs
that merely restate the document. Keep an anecdote, detour, fragment, or
unusual sequence when it carries context, tension, character, or a needed
reading effect.
