\section{Sources and acknowledgments}
\label{app:sources}

This article draws on five agent-facing resources in the
\href{https://github.com/msimchowitz/writing-skills}{writing-skills}
repository: Humanizer version 2.14.0, general-writing, better-usage,
writing-cadence, and academic-voice.

Humanizer builds on Siqi Chen's
\href{https://github.com/blader/humanizer}{humanizer}, which draws its pattern
catalog from Wikipedia's
\href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing}{Signs of AI
writing}. The general-writing framework builds on Peter Yang's
\href{https://github.com/petergyang/no-ai-slop}{no-ai-slop}. Both upstream
projects are distributed under the MIT License, and their license notices
remain with the corresponding skills. The cadence discussion also draws on
\href{https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/sentence_variety/index.html}{Purdue
OWL's account of sentence variety} and
\href{https://www.americanscientist.org/blog/the-long-view/the-science-of-scientific-writing}{Gopen
and Swan's analysis of reader expectations in scientific prose}.

Better-usage supplies the analysis of semantically compatible subjects, verbs,
and objects, including the need to name a missing intermediate relation.
Academic-voice supplies the expository stance: careful about evidence,
precise about relations, and written for a reader rather than in corporate or
social-media shorthand.

The causal hypotheses in \cref{sec:hypotheses} are conjectures presented for
discussion and testing. They should not be attributed to the upstream
projects.
