\section{Sources and acknowledgments}
\label{app:sources}

This article synthesizes Humanizer version 2.15.0 with focused skills for
general revision, semantic usage, cadence, academic voice, research abstracts,
paper writing, and whole-draft transitions from the
\href{https://github.com/msimchowitz/writing-skills}{writing-skills}
repository.

Humanizer builds on Siqi Chen's
\href{https://github.com/blader/humanizer}{humanizer}, whose pattern catalog
draws from Wikipedia's
\href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing}{Signs of AI
writing}. The general-writing framework builds on Peter Yang's
\href{https://github.com/petergyang/no-ai-slop}{no-ai-slop}. Both upstream
projects are distributed under the MIT License, and their license notices
remain with the corresponding skills.

The cadence discussion draws on
\href{https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/sentence_variety/index.html}{Purdue
OWL's account of sentence variety} and
\href{https://www.americanscientist.org/blog/the-long-view/the-science-of-scientific-writing}{Gopen
and Swan's analysis of reader expectations in scientific prose}. The
discussion of research voice and abstracts also uses the local conventions
distilled from the author's example papers in the paper-writing and
abstract-writing skills.

The causal accounts in \cref{sec:hypotheses} are hypotheses proposed in this
article. They do not represent positions taken by the upstream projects.
