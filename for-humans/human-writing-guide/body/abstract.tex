A technically correct paper can remain difficult to evaluate when its central
claim is separated from the evidence that supports it and the conditions under
which it holds. Effective paper writing begins with a claim--evidence map, then
carries those relationships into the order of sections, figures, and
sentences. This guide shows researchers how to make that argument visible
without changing its technical meaning or flattening the authors' voice. The
finished paper should let a careful reader identify the central claim, inspect
its support, and understand the conditions under which it applies.
