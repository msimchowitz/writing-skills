\section{Paper-planning sheet}
\label{app:planning}

Complete this sheet before drafting a new paper or restructuring an existing
one. Keep each answer short enough that coauthors can disagree with it
precisely.

\subsection{The paper contract}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Problem:} What practical or scientific problem does the paper
    address?

    \vspace{0.45cm}\hrule\vspace{0.35cm}

    \item \textbf{Gap:} Why does the strongest current method or explanation
    leave the problem unresolved?

    \vspace{0.45cm}\hrule\vspace{0.35cm}

    \item \textbf{Central claim:} What does the paper establish?

    \vspace{0.45cm}\hrule\vspace{0.35cm}

    \item \textbf{Scope and limitation:} Under what conditions does the claim
    hold, and where does it stop?

    \vspace{0.45cm}\hrule\vspace{0.35cm}

    \item \textbf{Reader consequence:} What should a careful reader understand
    or do differently after seeing the evidence?

    \vspace{0.45cm}\hrule
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Figure sequence}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Figure 1:} What is new, what changes, and why might it change?

    \vspace{0.35cm}\hrule\vspace{0.3cm}

    \item \textbf{Headline evidence:} Which figure or table establishes the
    central result?

    \vspace{0.35cm}\hrule\vspace{0.3cm}

    \item \textbf{Mechanism or theory:} Which visual or theorem resolves the
    next question?

    \vspace{0.35cm}\hrule\vspace{0.3cm}

    \item \textbf{Boundary:} Which result shows a failure, sensitivity, or
    limit?

    \vspace{0.35cm}\hrule
\end{enumerate}
